01 What is MLOps? — Formal Definition

MLOps is not a tool or a product. It's a paradigm — best practices + concepts + culture — for taking ML from notebook to production reliably and repeatably.

“MLOps (Machine Learning Operations) is a paradigm, including aspects like best practices, sets of concepts, as well as a development culture when it comes to the end-to-end conceptualization, implementation, monitoring, deployment, and scalability of machine learning products. Most of all, it is an engineering practice that leverages three contributing disciplines: machine learning, software engineering (especially DevOps), and data engineering.” Kreuzberger, Kühl, Hirschl — Machine Learning Operations (MLOps): Overview, Definition, and Architecture (2022)
GOAL

Productionize ML

Bridge the Dev↔Ops gap so ML proofs of concept actually reach production and stay there.

SCOPE

End-to-end

Conceptualization → implementation → monitoring → deployment → scalability of the ML product.

CULTURE

Product-oriented

Shift away from model-driven thinking; treat ML systems as products owned by cross-functional teams.

Why it matters (and why it usually fails)

  • Manual workflows. Data scientists still operate ML by hand → fragile production behavior.
  • Silos. Data, modelling, infra, and ops live in different teams with different stacks.
  • Hidden technical debt. Pipelines accrue undocumented dependencies, skews, and stale features.
  • No feedback loop. Models drift in production with nobody noticing until business KPIs collapse.

14 Open Challenges

ORGANIZATIONAL

Mindset & culture

Shift from model-centric to product-centric. Break data-science silos; convince stakeholders that MLOps maturity yields business gains.

ORGANIZATIONAL

Skills & education

Shortage of architects, data engineers, ML/DevOps engineers. MLOps is rarely part of academic curricula.

ML SYSTEM

Fluctuating demand

Training jobs have spiky CPU/RAM/GPU needs and varying data volumes — infra must scale elastically.

OPERATIONAL

Manual operation in prod

Heterogeneous stacks make manual ops error-prone. Continuous incoming data forces frequent retraining.

OPERATIONAL

Governance of artifacts

Strong versioning of data, models, and code is needed for reproducibility, auditability, and incident root-cause analysis.

OPERATIONAL

Multi-party support

Failures span ML, infra, and software components — root-causing requires cross-team observability.
